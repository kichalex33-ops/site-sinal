Summary

Between October and December 1977, residents of Vigia, Colares and Santo Antônio do Tauá, on Marajó Bay in northern Brazil, reported an unusual sequence of nighttime lights over the region. The Brazilian Air Force responded with a formal two-phase operation, commanded by Brigadier Protásio Lopes de Oliveira and coordinated on the ground by Captain Uyrangê Hollanda, gathering civilian statements, photographs and occurrence forms. Unlike most cases in this archive, here the existence of the investigation itself has never been disputed: the complete documentary fund — 758 items including reports, photos, press clippings, videos and audio produced by the Air Force between 1964 and 1985 — is today digitized and publicly available at Brazil's National Archive. What remains open is the physical origin of the reported phenomena; much of the popular narrative that circulates about the case was built decades later, without relying directly on that archive.

Hypotheses

All in the same field of evaluation. None wins by default.

Conventional military action

The operation itself is a documented fact, commanded by Brigadier Protásio Lopes de Oliveira, with Captain Uyrangê Hollanda coordinating on the ground.

Atmospheric phenomenon

Some civilian accounts are consistent with unidentified luminous phenomena, with no period instrumental analysis available to allow a confident classification today.

Extraordinary hypothesis

Often associated with the case in popular culture, but the available documents prove the investigation took place, not the origin of the phenomena reported.

Contradictions
  • The existence of the operation is a fact; the popular interpretation of what it investigated (and what it supposedly concluded) often goes beyond what the documents state.
  • Much of the narrative that circulates publicly about the case was built decades later, without relying directly on the National Archive's documentary fund.
What we know
  • The Brazilian Air Force conducted a formal two-phase operation between October and December 1977.
  • 758 documents from that period are preserved, digitized and publicly accessible at Brazil's National Archive.
What we don't know
  • The physical origin of the phenomena that prompted the operation.
  • The full content of every document in the fund, which requires direct research in the archive.
What we'd need to know
  • A systematic review of the complete documentary fund (SIAN, National Archive).
  • Cross-referencing the civilian accounts collected at the time against the operation's official records.
